Cedar Hollow Family Services is a fictional agency. Every person, quote and figure in this case study is illustrative.

## At a *glance*

- **41%** less time on case notes, from 2.6 to 1.5 hours a day per caseworker
- **88%** of visit notes finished within 24 hours, up from 46%
- **2 days** month-end notes backlog, down from 9

## The agency

Cedar Hollow Family Services is a mid-sized family services agency covering three rural counties. Its 38 caseworkers and 4 supervisors work with about 1,150 active families. Home visits are the core of the work, and most caseworkers drive 40 minutes or more between visits.

## The *problem*

Caseworkers spent about 2.6 hours a day writing case notes, by the agency's own time study in January. Only 46% of visit notes were finished within 24 hours of the visit, and the notes backlog averaged 9 days at the end of each month, at its worst before audits.

> People were writing notes in their cars, at lunch, at their kitchen tables at 10 pm.
>
> Program director, Cedar Hollow Family Services (fictional)

Supervisors spent Friday afternoons chasing late notes instead of coaching.

## What they *did*

Cedar Hollow rolled out Case Amplify over four months, starting in February.

1. **Pilot.** One team of 8 caseworkers started in month 1, two more teams joined in month 2, and everyone was using it by month 3.
2. **Dictate.** After a visit, the caseworker dictates what happened on their phone, and CARA drafts the note in the agency's own template.
3. **Review.** The caseworker reads the draft, corrects anything it got wrong or left out, and approves it. Nothing is saved until they do.
4. **Supervise.** Supervisors see a review queue of approved notes instead of a list of missing ones.

Each team had two hours of training and a "note buddy" for the first month. No new staff were hired during the rollout.

## The *results*

After four months, the same time study showed caseworkers spending 1.5 hours a day on notes, down from 2.6: 41% less time. Notes finished within 24 hours rose from 46% to 88%, and the month-end backlog fell from 9 days to 2. Home visits per caseworker rose from 7.2 to 8.6 a week.

| Month | Hours a day on notes | Notes done within 24 hours |
|---|---|---|
| January (before) | 2.6 | 46% |
| February | 2.4 | 58% |
| March | 2.0 | 71% |
| April | 1.7 | 82% |
| May | 1.5 | 88% |

> I used to write notes in the car. Now I read a draft, fix what it got wrong, and sign it. The note is still mine.
>
> Dana Ruiz, caseworker (fictional)

> Fridays used to be for chasing late notes. Now I spend them coaching.
>
> Marcus Bell, program supervisor (fictional)

## What made it *work*

- **People decide.** CARA drafts; the caseworker reviews, corrects and approves every note.
- **Their template, not a new one.** Drafts follow the agency's own note template, so supervisors review what they already know.
- **A slow start.** One pilot team first, a note buddy on every team, and two hours of training.
