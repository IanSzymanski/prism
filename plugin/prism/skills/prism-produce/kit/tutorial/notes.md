# Harbor Point: call notes for the case study

ILLUSTRATIVE SAMPLE for the Prism tutorial. Harbor Point is a fictional regional field services company. Every person, quote and figure below is invented for the example.

Call with the operations director and a crew lead, notes typed up afterwards.

## The organization

- Harbor Point, a regional field services company covering three coastal towns
- 38 field technicians, 4 crew leads, about 1,150 active customer sites
- Most technicians drive 40+ minutes between jobs

## The problem before

- Technicians spent about 2.6 hours a day writing up job reports (their own time study, January)
- Only 46% of job reports were finished within 24 hours of the job
- The report backlog averaged 9 days at the end of each month
- Director: "People were writing reports in their vans, at lunch, at their kitchen tables at 10 pm."
- Crew leads spent Friday afternoons chasing late reports instead of coaching

## What they did

- rolled out a new reporting tool over four months, starting Feburary
- Month 1: one pilot crew of 8 technicians; month 2: two more crews; month 3: everyone
- After a job, the technician dictates what happened on their phone; the tool drafts the report in the companys own template
- The technician reads the draft, corrects anything it got wrong or left out, and approves it. Nothing is saved until they do
- Two hours of training per crew; a "report buddy" on each crew for the first month
- No new staff were hired during the rollout

## Results after four months (May, same time study)

| Month | Hours a day on reports | Reports done within 24 hours |
|---|---|---|
| January (before) | 2.6 | 46% |
| February | 2.4 | 58% |
| March | 2.0 | 71% |
| April | 1.7 | 82% |
| May | 1.5 | 88% |

- Time on reports down 41% (2.6 hours to 1.5 hours a day per technician)
- Reports done within 24 hours: 46% to 88%
- Month-end backlog: 9 days to 2 days
- Jobs per technician per week: 7.2 to 8.6
- crew lead said turnover is down too
- director said staff call it "the best tool we have ever bought"

- Lee Park, crew lead: "Fridays used to be for chasing late reports. Now I spend them coaching."
