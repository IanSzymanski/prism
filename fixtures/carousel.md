# Using AI in casework, *responsibly*

AI can take real weight off documentation. These habits keep people, and their judgment, in charge.
::::

:::: {.post .panel burst="0.6" note="02 / 07"}
[01]{.num}

## AI drafts. *People decide.*

Eligibility, safety, placement and service decisions stay with qualified staff. AI can summarize and suggest; it doesn't get the final say.
::::

:::: {.post .panel burst="0.7" note="03 / 07"}
[02]{.num}

## Know what it *gets wrong*

::: checks
- Mistakes and omissions
- Details the source never said
- Misread context
- Confident wording about uncertain facts
:::
::::

:::: {.post .panel burst="0.35" note="04 / 07"}
[03]{.num}

## Check it against *the source*

Before you approve a note or summary, trace each fact back to the conversation, form or record it came from.
::::

:::: {.post .panel burst="0.66" note="05 / 07"}
[04]{.num}

## Keep client data in *approved tools*

Don't paste case details into personal chat apps. Use the tools your organization has approved for sensitive information.
::::

:::: {.post .panel burst="0.3" note="06 / 07"}
[05]{.num}

## Correct it. Don’t just *accept\ it.*

::: steps
1. **Review** the draft
2. **Correct** what's off
3. **Approve** what's right
:::

Editing a draft is the job working as designed, not a sign the tool failed.
